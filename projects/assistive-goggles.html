Assistive goggles

A wearable prototype for people with visual impairments. Ultrasonic sensors measure the distance to obstacles and firmware turns it into audio cues.

    Define

    Warn the wearer about obstacles ahead

    A wearable assistive navigation prototype for people with visual impairments. It detects obstacles in front of the wearer in real time and tells them through sound.

    Build

    An Arduino, ultrasonic rangefinders and an earpiece

    An Arduino microcontroller reads ultrasonic rangefinders mounted on the goggles. Each sensor sends out a pulse of sound and times how long the echo takes to come back.

    The C++ firmware converts that time of flight into a distance. Drag the obstacle to see the numbers it works with.

    Echo comes back after

    Distance = speed of sound × time ÷ 2, with sound at 343 m/s. The pulse on screen is slowed down so you can see it.

    An audio feedback loop through an integrated earpiece turns obstacle proximity into real-time cues the wearer can navigate by.

    Test and iterate

    Filtering out noise and false triggers

    Ultrasonic readings get messy when the sensor is moving. The firmware filters out sensor noise and false triggers during movement, so the wearer only hears about obstacles that are really there.
