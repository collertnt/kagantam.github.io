How I work: the engineering design process

Every project on this site went through the same loop. Here is each stage, with where it showed up in real work.

Define the need

Write the problem down as something to achieve, before deciding what to build.

  • Driving range coverA golf course wanted members practising through rain and snow. The design, materials, cost and build timing were all still open.
  • Digital manometerThe need is "measure and log a pressure difference", not "build an Arduino gadget". That kept other sensors on the table.

Research

Find out what already exists and where the limits are.

  • Driving range cover26 sources behind the final report, and 30+ material and bill-of-materials options compared.
  • Digital manometerSensor datasheet first, because it sets every limit. Then the fluids theory, then existing builds.

Specify requirements

Turn each need into a number and a way to check it.

  • Digital manometer12 requirements, each with a target and a test. Accuracy within ±15 Pa. Total cost within $150 CAD.
  • Driving range coverEvery design judged on the same four things: cost, lifespan, weather and build time.

Brainstorm concepts

Come up with more than one answer before picking.

  • Driving range coverThree different structures in SolidWorks: a tensile canopy, a metal roof and a portable cover.
  • Digital manometerThree ways to sense pressure, plus separate options for the display and the ports.

Select a concept

Choose with a method I can show to someone else.

  • Digital manometerA weighted decision matrix, with the weights agreed before scoring. The winner scored 4.40 out of 5 against 3.30 and 2.75.
  • Driving range coverRecommended the metal roof: the highest cost, but a 50 to 70 year life and the best in BC snow and wind.

Develop and build

Detail the design, then make it.

  • Driving range cover5+ iterations of each design in SolidWorks.
  • Assistive gogglesAn Arduino, ultrasonic sensors and C++ firmware in a wearable prototype.
  • Formula NanoMechanisms for a custom probing tool.

Test and iterate

Check it against the requirements. Change one thing. Check again.

  • Assistive gogglesFiltered out the sensor noise and false triggers that showed up during movement.
  • Driving range coverPresented to the general manager, then updated the final report with his feedback.
  • Digital manometerTest plan written before the build, one test per requirement.

Communicate the result

Write it up so someone else can act on it.

  • Driving range coverA 20-page report, presented to the client's general manager in September 2025.
  • Digital manometerThe design report follows this same loop, section by section.

How I got into engineering

It started with LEGO. I'd build the set on the box, take it apart, then build whatever I wanted out of the pieces. The fun part was always figuring out why something held together, or why it didn't.

In high school I joined a VEX Robotics team. We won multiple awards and finished top 8 at Provincials in the Skills component. It was the first time I worked on something technical with a team under real pressure, and it's a big part of why I chose engineering.

From the bench to MVP

When I made my club team, it was ranked in the top 20 in Canada, and I barely played. Most matches I watched from the bench.

So I tried to learn as much as I could. I reached out to specific coaches and asked them to teach me. At my games, I'd ask coaches to watch just a point or two and give me a quick pointer afterward. I watched a lot of game film, of good players and of myself.

It took a while, but practice turned into minutes, and minutes turned into a starting spot. In 2025 I was named MVP and got the Oakville Thunder Coaches' Choice Award.

In 2026 I captained Iroquois Ridge to the Halton Sr. Boys Tier 1 championship and was named the team's 2025–26 MVP. I also went to Volleyball Canada Nationals in Calgary with my club team.

What it taught me

Sitting on the bench taught me to stay ready and keep working when nobody's watching. Being captain taught me to look out for the guys who were where I used to be. A team is only as good as how much the players trust each other, and that's true off the court too.
