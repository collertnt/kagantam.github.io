Engineering internship · Shuswap Lake Golf Course, Blind Bay BC

Designing a year-round driving range cover

The Shuswap Lake Golf Course asked for a covered structure over its 90 ft × 18 ft driving range. Rain and snow made the range unusable for part of the year, and the goal was to extend the season for members. As Research & Design Engineer, I took the project from first concept to final technical sign-off.

The client needed five things: a design concept, a materials choice with reasons, a construction timeline, a budget, and the best time of year to build without disrupting golfers. I delivered a 20-page engineering report with three SolidWorks designs and 5+ iterations each, then presented it to the general manager and updated the final report with the client's feedback.

A galvanized steel frame with a heavy-duty, UV-resistant PVC tarp that can be replaced. Quick to install and affordable.

The road to the court

From the end of the bench to MVP

My volleyball story isn't about talent. It's about earning a spot. I started out barely playing, and I worked my way into a starting role by asking for help, studying the game, and staying ready.

The beginning

On the bench of a top-20 team in Canada

I made a club team ranked among the top 20 in the country, and I spent most matches on the bench. I didn't get much playing time, but I got a close look at how elite players train, communicate, and compete.

The grind

Asking for feedback

I contacted specific coaches to learn from them. I asked coaches to watch just a point or two of my games and give me quick pointers afterward. Short, honest feedback I could use the next day.

The film room

Studying the game and myself

I watched a lot of game film, both high-level volleyball and my own matches, to find my mistakes and figure out how to fix them before the next practice.

2025

Earned a starting role, then MVP

Practice turned into playing time, and playing time turned into a starting spot. In 2025 I was named MVP and received the Oakville Thunder 2024–25 Coaches' Choice Award.

2026

Captain, Halton champion, MVP, Nationals

As captain, I led Iroquois Ridge to the Halton Senior Boys Tier 1 championship and was named MVP. That same season I competed at Volleyball Canada Nationals in Calgary with my club team. I also worked as an assistant coach for my school team, helping design practices and analyze game film.

What volleyball taught me

Leadership

Being captain meant setting the standard in practice, keeping the team calm in close sets, and lifting up the players who were where I used to be.

Teamwork

Volleyball is a sport where no one can touch the ball twice in a row. Every point depends on trusting the people next to you.

Work ethic

Nobody gave me minutes. I earned them through extra reps, film sessions, and treating every practice like a tryout.

Resilience

Starting on the bench could have made me quit. Instead, it made me ask better questions and work harder to improve.
